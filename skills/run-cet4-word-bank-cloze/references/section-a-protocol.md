# CET4 word-bank-cloze protocol

## Material intake

Require:

- complete passage with all blank numbers;
- complete A–O word bank;
- year, month, set/source label;
- opening words as fingerprint;
- answer key only after baseline evidence is frozen.

If the set number is uncertain, identify the material by opening words and items rather than guessing.

## Baseline capture

Allow the learner to choose a natural procedure. Do not impose the protocol before the first clean baseline. For intervention trials, state the selected behavior change in advance without teaching passage content.

Capture:

- first-pass topic and stance model;
- point at which the passage became coherent;
- order of blanks answered;
- provisional versus committed choices;
- words considered for each uncertain blank;
- revisions and forced choices;
- final independent reread or its absence.

## Candidate-bank map

For each candidate, record only what is independently available before answering:

- surface form;
- likely lemma;
- possible part(s) of speech;
- visible morphology;
- known contextual senses;
- confidence.

Do not force one translation before reading the sentence. One form may have multiple senses or parts of speech.

## Slot signature

For every consequential blank, reconstruct:

`part of speech + required form + syntactic role + valency/complement + collocation + contextual sense + cohesion + discourse stance`

Use a shorter signature during timed trials and the full signature during diagnosis.

### Quick timed signature

1. What part of speech is required?
2. What visible form is required?
3. What words govern or complete the blank?
4. What meaning and stance must the sentence express?

### Deep diagnostic signature

1. Identify finite predicates, auxiliaries, and copulas.
2. Match each predicate to its subject.
3. Mark clause boundaries and connectors.
4. Restore reduced relatives, ellipsis, or omitted repeated material.
5. Resolve pronoun and demonstrative antecedents.
6. Determine the blank’s syntactic role and morphology.
7. Test verb valency, complements, prepositions, and stable chunks.
8. Test precise contextual meaning.
9. Test lexical and grammatical cohesion with adjacent sentences.
10. Test agreement with the paragraph and passage model.

## Candidate selection

Require four independent checks:

1. **Form:** part of speech and morphology fit.
2. **Frame:** syntax, valency, complement, and collocation fit.
3. **Meaning:** the restored proposition is precise.
4. **Discourse:** cohesion, stance, and passage logic fit.

Do not accept a candidate solely because:

- its broad Chinese meaning sounds relevant;
- it is the only remaining option;
- a neighboring keyword feels positive or negative;
- another blank has already consumed a better candidate.

## Passage-model update

Treat the first-pass interpretation as provisional. When a later paragraph clarifies topic, chronology, stance, referent, or contrast:

1. record the model update;
2. return to earlier provisional blanks;
3. reread the complete restored sentences;
4. revise only with a stated constraint.

Measure whether this rescan actually occurred.

## Final validation

Before submission:

- read each restored sentence independently;
- confirm every chosen letter is used once at most;
- inspect direct swaps and cascades;
- revisit answers based mainly on elimination;
- distinguish “candidate recognized” from “candidate correctly mapped.”

## Answer-topology measures

Report:

- positional accuracy: correct blanks / 10;
- candidate-set overlap: reference answers present anywhere in the learner’s selected set / 10;
- missed correct candidates;
- selected distractors;
- misplaced correct candidates;
- direct swaps;
- cascade length after the first allocation error;
- grammatically invalid final placements;
- answers without independent rereading.

These measures distinguish lexical availability from placement and validation ability.

## Explanation order after the baseline

For each consequential blank:

1. quote the complete local sentence and necessary adjacent context;
2. restore the sentence skeleton;
3. state the slot signature;
4. compare the learner’s candidate with the correct candidate;
5. identify why the learner’s actual cue was insufficient;
6. classify the mechanism;
7. extract only the reusable word, chunk, frame, or reading action;
8. decide whether the item changes a bottleneck or stays in the backlog.

Do not turn every item into a separate training assignment.
